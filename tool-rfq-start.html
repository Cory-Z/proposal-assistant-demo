Start from an RFQ

Turn a new RFQ into a summary, a pre-filled SME intake, questions for the client and a draft email to send them. Claude does the reading, so this page builds the request for you to paste into Claude. Nothing you type here is saved or sent anywhere. In this portfolio demo there is no Claude behind the prompt, so a sample reply for a made-up RFQ is included at the bottom of the page.

1. Tell Claude about the RFQ

Attaching the file is better for long RFQs and tables. Do not include real patient data.

Open Claude and start a Cowork session (not a regular chat). If your RFQ is a file, attach it. Paste this in and send. Claude will ask you to approve the Proposal Index folder; approve it.

Open Claude

  






  
2. What Claude sends back

  
    
    
  1. RFQ at a glance. A short summary: client, product, intended users, what is being asked, key dates, how the proposal must be submitted.
    2. 
    
  2. Pre-filled SME intake. One line that starts with RFQ-PREFILL. It carries every answer the RFQ states, such as device type, study type and user groups.
    3. 
    
  3. Questions for the client. Only what the RFQ leaves open and that changes scope or price, with the assumption we will use if they do not answer.
    4. 
    
  4. A draft email to the client with those questions, ready to edit and send.
    5. 
    
  5. Flags and similar proposals. Anything unusual (tight dates, heavy contract terms) and the 3 closest past proposals with links.
    6. 
  






  
3. What to do with it

  
    
    
  1. Copy the RFQ-PREFILL line from Claude's reply.
    2. 
    
  2. Open the SME intake, paste the line into Start from an RFQ at the top, and click Fill from RFQ. Check every answer marked as pre-filled and fix anything wrong. The SME still answers whatever the RFQ did not say.
    3. 
    
  3. Edit the draft email and send it to the client yourself. Claude never sends anything.
    4. 
    
  4. When the SME intake is complete, continue with the Comm Ops intake as usual.
    5. 
  

  

  
Everything Claude produces here is a first pass for a Acme HF reviewer to check, including anything about regulations or clinical details. It comes from the RFQ text only.





  
Demo: a sample reply for a made-up RFQ

  
In the real tool, Claude reads the client's RFQ and sends back the pieces below. This one is invented (a fictional client, device and RFQ) so you can try the rest of the flow.

  
RFQ at a glance
    
    
  • Client: Northwind Devices (stated). Product: PenFlow autoinjector for adult self-injection (stated).
    • 
    
  • Ask: a human factors validation study with 15 nurses, 15 patients and 8 caregivers, plus a use specification and review of the client's use-related risk analysis (stated).
    • 
    
  • Use environments: home and pharmacy (stated). Pilot session expected (inferred from the study plan).
    • 
    
  • Dates: project start in January 2027, finish by May 2027. Proposal due in two weeks, submitted by email (stated).
    • 
    
  • Pricing format: one fixed fee per deliverable (stated).
    • 
  

  
Questions for the client, with the assumption if they do not answer
    
    
  1. Affects price. How long is each session, and is training provided before it? Assumption: 1 hour for nurses, 1.5 hours for patients, no training.
    2. 
    
  2. Affects price. Who recruits the participants? Assumption: we recruit.
    3. 
    
  3. Affects scope. Is the URRA final or still changing? Assumption: final, 50 to 125 rows.
    4. 
    
  4. Good to confirm. Will the device be production-equivalent at the time of the study? Assumption: yes.
    5. 
  

  
Draft email to the client
Subject: Northwind Devices PenFlow RFQ: a few questions

Hello [Name],

Thank you for the RFQ for the PenFlow human factors validation study. To give you an accurate scope and price, could you confirm:

1. Session length for each user group, and whether participants are trained before the session.
2. Whether you would like us to recruit participants.
3. Whether the use-related risk analysis is final, and roughly how many rows it has.
4. Whether the device will be production-equivalent at the time of the study.

If we do not hear back by [date], we will assume 1-hour sessions for nurses and caregivers, 1.5 hours for patients, no training, that we recruit, a final risk analysis of 50 to 125 rows, and a production-equivalent device.

Best regards,
[Name]
